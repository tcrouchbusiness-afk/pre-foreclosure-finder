# Legal guardrails

Not legal advice. Written for Florida (2026-10). Other states have their own versions; check them during onboarding.

- **F.S. 501.1377 (Foreclosure-related rescue transactions / "equity purchasers")** heavily regulates buying from an owner in foreclosure: written contract requirements, a **5-business-day rescission**, a ban on leaseback/repurchase schemes and on collecting fees up front. Have a Florida real-estate attorney approve your purchase contract and scripts.
- **Calls/texts**: federal TCPA and DNC, plus the **Florida Telephone Solicitation Act (F.S. 501.059)**, which has strict rules on autodialed calls and texts. Scrub DNC and don't autodial or mass-text without written consent. Direct mail is the safest first channel.
- **Bankruptcy**: once a suggestion of bankruptcy is filed, stop and get counsel.
- **Public records use**: these are public records. Scrape gently, honor the site terms, and don't redistribute bulk data.

---

## Also
- **Probate**: a personal representative needs court authority (or the will's power of sale) to sell estate property. Heirs of a protected homestead may own it directly. Work through the estate attorney.
- **Unknown tenants**: if a tenant is in place, the owner or estate is a landlord. Florida tenant protections apply after a foreclosure sale.
- **Data handling**: the workbook holds names, addresses, and phone numbers. Keep it local; don't publish or resell it.
